# STORYBOARD & IMAGE DIRECTION — Robin Explainer

Scene-by-scene visuals to pair with `talk-track.md`. Read this before writing prompts; the style
rules at the top are what keep eight scenes looking like one video.

---

## The controlling metaphor

**A library, and the workshop that stocks it.**

One metaphor, held for the entire runtime. Every abstraction maps to a physical object:

| The real thing | What the viewer sees |
|---|---|
| Plan documents, packets, help articles | A messy stack of paper on a desk |
| The Knowledge Factory (content-cleaner) | A small workshop / conveyor: paper in, cards out |
| A cleaned KB article | One clean index card on a shelf |
| The human approval gate | A hand placing a stamp on a card |
| Coverage flags ("we don't cover that") | A card with a corner deliberately left blank |
| Robin's system prompt | A handwritten job card pinned above the desk |
| Robin | A warm desk lamp + telephone, always lit — **never a face, never a robot** |
| A call | A single glowing line between a house and the desk |
| The dashboard | A wall of small report cards, most green, a few marked |
| A coverage gap | An order slip on a hook, traveling back to the workshop |
| Scale (150 plans) | The same shelf repeated into the distance |

**Robin is never anthropomorphized.** No robot, no avatar, no assistant-with-a-face. She's a lamp, a
phone, and a job card. This is deliberate: the second you draw a robot, a non-technical viewer stops
hearing "system that follows rules" and starts hearing "the thing that replaces people."

---

## Style bible

- **Palette:** warm paper white, kraft/manila tan, walnut brown, one accent — a soft amber lamp
  glow. One cool accent (dusty teal) reserved *only* for the loop/feedback arrows.
- **Look:** tactile stop-motion / paper-craft / soft 3D isometric. Shallow depth of field. Soft
  overhead light with real shadows. Slightly imperfect — hand-made, not rendered.
- **Camera:** locked-off or a very slow push-in. No swooping, no whip pans.
- **People:** hands only, never faces. A hand stamping a card, a hand holding a phone. Keeps it
  universal and sidesteps the uncanny valley entirely.
- **Text in frame:** none from the generator. All on-screen text is added in the edit — generated
  text renders as garbage and instantly reads as AI.
- **Hard bans:** robots, humanoid AI, glowing brains, blue circuit boards, binary rain, neural-net
  node graphs, holograms, floating UI panels, lens flare, HUD overlays.

## Scene-by-scene

### Scene 1 — The question (0:00–0:20)
**Visual:** A dark kitchen. One lamp on. A phone face-up on the counter beside a mug and an unopened
envelope from the plan. Nobody in frame. The clock reads 9:04.
**Motion:** Almost none. A very slow push-in on the phone.
**Feeling:** Quiet, a little stuck. This is the only "before" shot — it has to earn the rest.
**Alt shot:** Same kitchen, an open laptop showing a login page (blurred, no readable text).

### Scene 2 — Where the answers live today (0:20–0:50)
**Visual:** Overhead on a desk. A thick spiral-bound packet, a fanned folder of documents, a printed
help article with a coffee ring, a sticky note. All of it *good* — well-kept, tabbed, cared for.
**Motion:** Overhead, papers sliding into a neat stack, then the stack getting taller than it should.
**Feeling:** Not chaos. Abundance in the wrong shape. The point is "correct but unreachable," not
"messy and bad."
**Second beat:** A single desk lamp switching off — the office closing.

### Scene 3 — The Knowledge Factory (0:50–1:25)
**Visual:** A small tabletop workshop. Paper feeds in one side of a simple wooden machine; clean
cream index cards come out the other, landing in a neat stack. Off to the side, a small bin of
discarded offcuts (the fee tables, the boilerplate).
**Motion:** The conveyor. Paper in, cards out. Rhythmic and satisfying.
**Beat 3b — the two rules (this is the trust shot):** Close on a single card. A **hand** enters
frame with a rubber stamp and presses it down. Next to it, a card with a blank corner and a small
tag — the "we don't cover this" flag.
**Feeling:** Craft. Somebody made this on purpose.

### Scene 4 — Robin's instructions (1:25–2:00)
**Visual:** A desk. A warm amber lamp, a telephone handset, and **pinned to the wall above it, a
handwritten job card** — five short lines. Do not render the text; the five lines are typed on in the
edit, one at a time, as the narrator reads them.
**Motion:** Slow push-in on the card. Each line types on in sync with the VO.
**Feeling:** This is the shot people will screenshot. Give it room.
**Note:** This is where the system prompt lives in the story — as *instructions*, never as "prompt."

### Scene 5 — The call gets answered (2:00–2:15)
**Visual:** Wide, from above. A small house on the left, the lamp-and-phone desk on the right, and a
single warm line of light connecting them. The line completes, holds, and the house's window
brightens.
**Motion:** The line drawing across, then a soft pulse.
**Feeling:** Relief. This is the payoff shot for Scene 1 — same kitchen light, now on.

### Scene 6 — The report card (2:15–2:45)
**Visual:** A wall or corkboard of small cards in a grid, each one a call. Most are marked with a
small green tick, a few with an amber mark, one or two with a red one.
**Motion:** Cards populating the grid one by one, marks appearing after each.
**Feeling:** Legible at a glance. A viewer should understand "these are grades" with no narration.
**Do NOT:** screen-record the real dashboard. Illustrate it. The real dashboard is a separate clip.

### Scene 7 — The loop closes (2:45–3:05)
**Visual:** From the report-card wall, the *marked* cards lift off, become small order slips, and
travel back along a **dusty-teal** line to the workshop from Scene 3. The workshop runs. A new card
comes out. A hand stamps it. It slots onto the shelf — and the shelf visibly gains a card.
**Motion:** The return journey. This is the single most important motion in the video — it has to
read as a *circle*, not a line.
**Feeling:** Click. The moment the puzzle closes.

### Scene 8 — The whole machine (3:05–3:20)
**Visual:** Pull all the way back. The full loop visible at once as a simple circular arrangement:
documents → workshop → shelf → desk lamp → report wall → back to workshop. Then keep pulling: the
same arrangement repeats into soft focus, again and again, into the distance.
**Motion:** One continuous slow pull-back. No cuts.
**Feeling:** "Oh — this is one thing, and it's designed to repeat."
**Final frame:** The loop, small and centered, with the on-screen text *Content in → calls out →
gaps back.*

## Production recommendation

**Don't generate all eight scenes as AI video.** Consistency across eight generated clips is the
hardest problem in this format, and it's the one that makes a video look cheap.

Do this instead:

1. Generate **one hero still** for Scene 3 first. Iterate until the palette, texture, and light are
   exactly right. That image becomes your **style reference for every other prompt** — attach it as a
   reference image so scenes 1–8 inherit the look.
2. Generate the remaining scenes as **stills** (fast, cheap, easy to re-roll).
3. Animate the stills in the edit with slow push-ins and parallax. For a 3-minute explainer at this
   pace, motion stills read as *deliberate*, not as a limitation.
4. Reserve true video generation for the **three shots where motion carries meaning**: the conveyor
   (Scene 3), the call connecting (Scene 5), and the loop closing (Scene 7).
5. Generate the VO in ElevenLabs and cut picture to the VO, not the other way around.

Budget-wise that's ~10 stills + 3 short video clips instead of 8 full generations, and it will look
markedly more coherent.
