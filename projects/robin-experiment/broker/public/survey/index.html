Asked at the end of a call: would you rather handle this with Robin, or hold for a person, even if it took longer?

Is the sample big enough?What it shows. How often Robin actually asked the survey, and the headline share redrawn one response at a time with its 95% range. The chart appears at 20 responses.

How to read it. The result is callable once the shaded band stops crossing 50%. At n=30 a 60/40 is noise. If Robin asked on fewer than 70% of eligible calls, every figure above rests on a biased slice.

Whether the headline can be trusted yet.Think of it as counting votes. Ballots arrive one at a time, Robin against A Person. After one ballot you would never call it. After ten you are tempted but know the next ten could go either way. After fifty you feel it settle.

The chart is that feeling, drawn. The line is Robin's share after each ballot. The shaded band is your honest doubt: given what has been counted, the final result could land anywhere in it. It is wide at the start and narrows as ballots pile up. The rule at 50% is a majority.

Calling it means the whole band is above 50, not just the line. If the bottom edge still dips under half, A Person could still win and you would be guessing. The page refuses to guess.

Nothing here is a new number. It is the headline share and its range from the top of the page, shown as they evolved instead of only as they stand today.
