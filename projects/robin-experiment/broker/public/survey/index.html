Asked at the end of a call: would you rather handle this with Robin, or hold for a person, even if it took longer?

Would they rather use Robin? What it shows. How many responses chose Robin over holding for a person, with a 95% range. Transferred calls and failed verifications are excluded. The recommendation bar splits promoters (9–10), passives (7–8) and detractors (0–6). Voice is the average 0–10 rating.

How to read it. A winner is called only when the range clears 50%. Read whether it says “holds,” not the decimal. Responses, not people: one tester with five calls is five responses.

Score vs. what they’d chooseWhat it shows. The 0–10 score someone gave Robin against what they said they would choose next time. One dot per call.

How to read it. The diagonal is expected. A promoter who still wants a person: trust isn’t there yet. A detractor who would still use Robin: convenience beats the flaws.

The score they gave against the choice they’d make. Watch the two corners.

Needs reviewWhat it shows. Calls with negative sentiment or a score of 6 or below, each opening to its transcript.

How to read it. Read these first. An empty list on a small sample means nothing.

Calls that went badly. Click one to read it.

What people saidWhat it shows. Every written comment, verbatim, one per call, grouped under themes a model found once there are enough comments. Each theme carries one quote; open it to read the comments behind it. Personal details are scrubbed.

How to read it. Two comments is two people. The themes are an index, not a finding; the comments are the evidence.

Is the sample big enough?What it shows. How often Robin actually asked the survey, and the headline share redrawn one respondent at a time with its 95% range. The chart appears at 20 respondents.

How to read it. The result is callable once the shaded band stops crossing 50%. At n=30 a 60/40 is noise. If Robin asked on fewer than 70% of eligible calls, every figure above rests on a biased slice.
