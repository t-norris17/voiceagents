Robin Survey Instrument

A two-question viability survey bolted onto a live ElevenLabs voice agent, designed to come back off cleanly. No new webhook, no workflow graph — three layers, two of which were already standing.

The shape of it

The survey is not a feature. It is a temporary measuring instrument for one question: would callers rather handle this with Robin, or hold for a person even if it takes longer. It runs on two cohorts, then comes off. That constraint drove every build decision below — most importantly, nothing was added that would be hard to remove.

Three layers

Asking

A paragraph in the system prompt

Not a tool, not a workflow node. Robin decides when to ask from written rules, the same way she decides when to transfer. A mid-call webhook tool would pay a round trip to capture something the transcript already holds, and add a fresh way for the conversation to break mid-survey. A workflow node would mean building a graph you then have to dismantle — and dismantling is exactly what makes a temporary instrument dangerous. A paragraph is removed by deleting a paragraph.

Capturing

Data Collection fields

After hangup, the analysis model reads the transcript and fills typed fields. Writing them through the connector also mints the matching analysis_items entries the dashboard reads, so no hand-entry in the Analysis tab. The write is a full replace, not a merge: the field list you send is the field list you get.

Storing

The post-call webhook that already existed

Nothing new to wire. The agent already posts transcript plus analysis to /api/postcall on the Vercel broker. The only new code is the survey parser inside that endpoint, which claude/robin-survey already wrote and which has not shipped.

What the survey asks

Two questions on a normal call. The same two before a transfer, which is the deliberate carve-out — a caller about to be handed to a human is the most informative person you can ask the preference question.

  • Satisfaction. "Quick one to five, how was that for you?" → satisfaction
  • Preference. "And if you had a question like this again, would you rather do it this way with me, or hold for a person, even if it took longer?" → prefer_agent

Plus three bookkeeping fields: survey_offered, survey_consent, and offer_context — which records in_call versus pre_transfer so the two moments stay separable in the results. Five fields dropped: understood, csat, improve_verbatim, callback_consent, callback_window.

What the tests found

Sixty simulated calls against the clone, twenty per scenario, judged against written success conditions. Repeats matter because "does she offer the survey" is probabilistic, not deterministic — one run gives you an anecdote, twenty give you a rate.

ScenarioPassedReal failuresReading
Fires before a transfer 17 / 20 3 Carve-out works, with a race — see below
Suppressed on failed verification 16 / 20 2 Leaks about one call in ten
Fires on a resolved call 3 / 20 0 All 17 failures were harness, not Robin

That last row needs the caveat spelled out or it reads as a disaster: twelve runs timed out waiting for Robin's next turn and five had the simulated caller hang up early. Every criterion that could be evaluated passed, with the judge quoting her verbatim. The twelve timeouts are themselves a finding — see the stale tools below.

Two defects the tests surfaced

The carve-out overrides the suppression 2 in 19

On a caller who failed verification twice, Robin ran the whole survey on the way into the transfer. The prompt says "before a transfer, ask the same two first" in one place and "do not ask when verification failed" in another, and the model picks the wrong one about one time in ten.

"I'm getting you to a specialist now — two quick things first, takes fifteen seconds?" … "on a scale of one to five, how was that for you?"

A rating from someone who never got service is unattributable and drags the denominator around. Fix is to make the suppression explicitly beat the carve-out rather than sitting in a separate list.

She offers, then transfers before collecting 3 in 20

Distinct from the first. Robin proposes the two questions, then fires transfer_to_number before asking them or before waiting for the answers. The judge: "The agent only asked one question and the user did not answer it before the transfer was initiated." The transfer instinct wins mid-sequence. The offer is spent and nothing is captured.

Confirmed live: the stale broker host

Across the sixty runs the agent called lumio-retirement.vercel.app 26 times — document_resolution 18, get_plan_details 7, send_reset_email once. get_plan_details is the one that matters: it returns {"found": false} with no error, and it is the prime suspect for the twelve 60-second turn timeouts on the resolved-call path. This is not a survey bug; it is the measurement environment being broken underneath the thing being measured.

How it comes off

Every agent update mints a new version, which is the removal plan: record the version before the survey goes on and the version after, revert when the customer wave closes. Branches exist too, but the live phone number is pinned to a branch and that interaction is unverified, so version rollback — which has been watched working — is the safer lever.
