# Vertex Manufacturing 401(k) — Loans From Your Account

**Recordkeeper:** NestEgg Retirement Services · **Plan:** Vertex Manufacturing 401(k) (Plan 001)

*Knowledge-base article for the voice agent. General plan rules — a participant's own eligibility (their vested balance and any existing loan) comes from their account, not this article. Synthetic demo content.*

## Can I take a loan against my 401(k)?

**Yes — the Vertex plan allows participant loans.** You borrow from your own vested balance and pay yourself back, with interest, through payroll deductions. It is not a taxable withdrawal as long as you repay it on schedule.

## How much you can borrow

**Minimum:** $1,000.

**Maximum:** the **lesser of $50,000 or 50% of your vested account balance** (the $50,000 cap is reduced by your highest loan balance in the past 12 months).

**One loan outstanding at a time.** You must pay off an existing loan before taking a new one.

## Terms and cost

**Repayment term:** up to **5 years** (up to **15 years** if the loan is to buy your primary residence).

**Interest rate:** **Prime + 1%**, fixed for the life of the loan. The interest you pay goes back into **your own account**.

**Fees:** a **$75 origination fee** and a **$25/year maintenance fee**, deducted from your account.

**Repayment:** automatic **after-tax payroll deductions**, usually starting the pay period after the loan is issued.

## Contributing while you have a loan

**Yes — you keep contributing while you repay a loan.** Your regular contributions continue at whatever rate you have set, and the **employer match continues** on those contributions. Loan repayments are a **separate, after-tax payroll deduction** that comes out on top of your contributions; they are **not** contributions, do **not** earn a match, and do **not** count toward the IRS annual contribution limit.

You can **change your contribution rate** while a loan is outstanding, including pausing contributions, but loan repayments continue regardless. Stopping contributions does not reduce or pause your loan payments.

## Paying a loan off early

**You can repay a loan early at any time, in full, with no prepayment penalty.** Request a payoff amount on the portal (**Loans → Pay Off Loan**) or from the participant line; the payoff figure includes interest accrued to the payoff date. Once the loan is paid off, you are eligible to request a new loan.

Partial extra payments are not accepted through payroll; early repayment is a single payoff of the full balance.

## Important things to know

**Loans reduce your invested balance** while outstanding, so that money isn't in the market.

**If you leave Vertex** with a loan outstanding, the remaining balance generally becomes due. If it isn't repaid, it's treated as a **loan offset** — taxable, and subject to a **10% early-withdrawal penalty if you're under 59½**. You can avoid the tax by rolling an amount equal to the offset into an IRA or new plan by your tax-filing deadline (including extensions).

Missing payments can cause the loan to **default** and become a taxable distribution.

## How to request a loan

Model and request a loan on the portal at **nesteggu.com** (Loans → Request a Loan), or call the **NestEgg participant line at 1-800-555-0148**. You'll see the amount available to you, the payment schedule, and the rate before you confirm.

## Common questions

*How much can I borrow?* Lesser of $50,000 or 50% of your vested balance.

*Can I have two loans?* No — one at a time on this plan.

*Can I still contribute while I'm paying a loan back?* Yes. Contributions and the match continue; loan payments are a separate after-tax deduction.

*Can I pay my loan off early?* Yes, in full, at any time, with no penalty. Then you can request a new loan.

*What if I leave with a loan?* It becomes due; unpaid amounts are offset and may be taxed/penalized.

*Is loan interest tax-deductible?* No — 401(k) loan interest is not deductible.
