Asking Robin a question without calling her

Type questions the way a member would, get the answers back, compare them to a written answer key. It is the fastest way to check her knowledge, and today it is reading from the wrong set of documents. That is the most important thing on this page.

the question tester 25 questions with a written answer key checked against the running system, 21 Sep 2026
01

The short version

Placing a phone call to check one wording is slow, and it ties up a tester. This does the same check in a few seconds, in a browser.

  • Paste questions, one per line. Each is answered the way Robin would answer it, using the same model she runs on and the same answering rules, at a setting that produces the same natural variation. Send the same question again and the wording will differ slightly, exactly as it would on two real calls.
  • Twenty-five of them have a written answer key. When a question matches one of those, the ideal answer appears beside what came back, so a reviewer can compare rather than remember.
  • You can flag anything that looks wrong and collect it for review, and you can see how long each answer took.
  • It is reading from our published articles, not from Robin's binder. These are supposed to be the same thing. Today they are not, and the gap is total rather than partial.

The thing to know before trusting it. Robin answers from six documents that were uploaded straight into ElevenLabs by hand. This tool answers from the 29 articles we have published through the Knowledge Factory. Not one of those 29 is one of her six. They belong to the earlier INTRUST plan and the newest was last changed on 27 July 2026, while Robin now answers for the Vertex Manufacturing plan. So today this tool tells you what a differently informed Robin would say. It is a working test of the pipeline, not a test of the live agent, and it becomes both the moment her documents are published through the factory.

02

What it is made of

  • Same model, same setting. It runs the model Robin runs, at a comparable setting, so the answers vary between sends the way hers vary between calls. That variation is the point rather than a nuisance: an answer that is right once and wrong the second time is a finding, and a tool that gave identical output every time would hide it.
  • Same answering rules. The instructions about leading with one sentence, declining to give investment advice, and saying she is not certain rather than filling a gap are shared with the live agent, so the shape and tone of the answers are representative.
  • The answer key is twenty-five questions, written by hand. It is a fixed list covering enrollment, contributions, the match, vesting, loans, withdrawals, rollovers, beneficiaries, account access and investments, plus one question she is supposed to refuse. Anything outside the list simply comes back without a comparison.
  • It falls back to a built-in copy of the knowledge when nothing at all has been published. That fallback is the reason the tool always answers something, and part of the reason it is easy not to notice what it is reading.
03

What happens to one question

  1. You type questions the way a member would ask them. Not keywords. The whole point is to see how a real phrasing lands.
  2. Each is sent on its own. No conversation, no memory between questions, so one answer cannot lean on the last.
  3. The published articles are loaded and held for a minute so a run of twenty questions does not fetch them twenty times. Publish something new and it appears here within the minute.
  4. The model answers under Robin's own rules, and returns the text and how long it took.
  5. If the question is one of the twenty-five, the written ideal answer is shown beside it, so a reviewer compares rather than judging from memory.
  6. Send it again to see the variation. Two runs of the same question show the spread a real caller would hear, which is the thing a single sample hides.
  7. Flag anything wrong and it collects into a list for review.
04

What it proves, and what it does not

What it does testand tests well

Whether the knowledge covers a question at all, which is the most common real failure. Whether the answer is phrased in a way that works spoken aloud to a member rather than read off a page. And whether it is steady: sending the same question twice shows the spread a real caller would hear, which a single sample hides completely.

What it never touchesby design

There is no phone call here. It does not exercise her voice, her turn-taking, or her handling of an interruption. It does not check identity first, because there is no caller to check, so it will happily answer a question Robin would have refused to answer until she had verified somebody. And it never looks up an account, so nothing involving a caller's own balance or loan can be tested this way. None of that is a defect. It is a knowledge test.

What it is reading todaythe part to fix

It grounds its answers in the articles we have published, which is correct in design and wrong in fact right now. Robin's six live documents were uploaded straight into ElevenLabs and never went through the Knowledge Factory, so our published set and her live set have nothing in common. Until that is fixed, a reviewer who finds a wrong answer here cannot conclude Robin would say the same thing, and a reviewer who finds a right answer cannot conclude she would either.

07

The choices behind this, and what each one cost

  • The same model, at the same setting. Answers vary between sends, which makes the tool honest about what a caller would actually hear. The cost is that it cannot be used as a pass-or-fail regression check, because two identical runs are supposed to differ.
  • It reads what we published, not what she serves. In the intended design these are the same thing, and reading our own copy is instant and free. The cost is exactly the situation we are in: when the two sets drift apart, the tool keeps working and keeps being wrong, with nothing on screen to say so.
  • It falls back rather than failing. With nothing published it answers from a built-in copy instead of returning an error. That was the right call for a demo and it is the wrong one now, because "it answered" and "it answered from the right thing" look identical.
  • An answer key for twenty-five questions only. Enough to cover the common ground without committing anybody to maintaining a key for everything. The cost is that the key is per-plan, and the current one belongs to the old plan.
  • No phone call, deliberately. A tester can check thirty wordings in the time one call takes. The cost is that everything about the call itself, the voice, the interruptions, the identity gate, is untested here and needs a real call.
08

What is true today, and what is not

It is answering from the wrong plan. We hold 29 published articles. Robin serves six documents. Checked by identifier, the overlap is zero. The published set belongs to the earlier INTRUST plan and the newest was last changed on 27 July 2026. This is not a subtle drift, it is two different bodies of knowledge.

Nothing on the page says so. The tool answers confidently either way, which is what makes this worth writing down. A reviewer using it this week would have no signal that the answers do not represent the live agent.

The page still names the old plan in its own text. It invites you to ask questions about the INTRUST plan, which is at least consistent with what it is reading, and is not what anyone opening it today expects.

The twenty-five test questions are the old plan's too. So the answer key beside each reply is a key for a plan Robin no longer serves.

The fix is one piece of work, not a rebuild. Publishing Robin's six live documents through the Knowledge Factory makes this tool accurate, gives her knowledge a dated audit trail for the first time, and fixes the reviewer's dependence on fetching her documents from ElevenLabs. One job, three modules improved.

The service behind it has no sign-in of its own. It is protected by being reached through the portal. It also spends money per question, so an open address would be worth closing before this is shared more widely.
