The Question Tester

Check her knowledge in a browser instead of on the phone.

Placing a call to check one wording is slow and ties up a tester. Paste questions the way a member would ask them and each is answered by the model Robin runs on, under her answering rules. Thirty wordings take about as long as one phone call.

How it works

  1. One question per line. Each is sent on its own with no memory between them, so one answer cannot lean on the last.
  2. Answers are grounded in the published articles, held for a minute so a run of twenty questions does not fetch them twenty times. Publish something new and it appears here within the minute.
  3. The model and the answering rules are Robin's. Send the same question twice and the wording differs, the way it would on two real calls. A single sample hides that spread.
  4. A curated set of questions has a written ideal answer. When yours matches one, the ideal appears beside the reply so a reviewer compares rather than judging from memory.
  5. Each answer shows its round-trip time, and anything that looks wrong can be flagged and collected for review.

What it tests

  • Whether the knowledge covers a question at all, which is the most common real failure.
  • Whether the answer works spoken aloud to a member, rather than read off a page.
  • Whether it is steady across repeat runs.

What it does not test

  • Her voice, her turn-taking, or how she handles an interruption. There is no call here.
  • The identity check. It answers straight away, where Robin would refuse until the caller was verified.
  • Anything involving a caller's own balance or loan, which needs an account lookup.
  • Whether Robin would answer the same way. It reads the articles published through the Knowledge Factory, so it speaks for her only where everything in her binder was published through it.
