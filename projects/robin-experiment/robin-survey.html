Would they rather use Robin?

Four questions at the end of a call, and five situations where she must not ask them at all. This is the instrument, the rules that keep it honest, how an answer becomes a number, and why it is designed to be taken back off.

01

The short version

This exists to settle one argument, and then to come off. It is not a feature and it is not meant to become one.

  • One question is the experiment. Would you rather handle a question like this with Robin, or hold for a person, even if it took longer? The clause about it taking longer is doing all the work. Without it people compare Robin to an imaginary human who picks up instantly, and the answer means nothing.
  • Three more questions ride along. How likely you would be to recommend handling a question this way, zero to ten. How natural her voice sounded, zero to ten. And an open invitation to say anything else.
  • She states it, she does not ask permission. "Before I go, please answer the following questions." Asking "do you have a moment?" invites a no that nobody needs, and these callers were told the questions were coming.
  • There are five situations where she says nothing at all. They are not soft guidance. They are the difference between a survey and an irritation.

Where it lives. There is no survey software. The questions are a paragraph in Robin's rules, the answers are pulled out of the transcript after the call by her own platform, and the results are read through the Quality page in the portal. Nothing was built that would be hard to remove, which was the point.

02

The four questions, and when she must not ask

  • A transfer she offered and they declined is not a transfer. The gate is about a call she is actually handing over. If she offered a person, they said no, and she finished the call herself, that is an ordinary completed call and it counts. Getting this wrong in either direction would bias the result: skipping those calls loses the people who considered a human and stayed, and asking on a real transfer irritates someone already being handed over.
  • Frustration is not a reason to skip. A caller unhappy with how it went, whose question she still answered, is exactly whose answer we need. She is instructed to ask them the same way she would ask anyone. This is the rule most likely to be quietly broken by a system trying to look good.
  • If they stop, she stops. Answers already given are kept. She never re-words a question and tries again, never asks why, and never pushes for more. Pushing is how a nine becomes a six.
  • She never reads the answers back. No summarising, no confirming what was recorded. And if somebody says something sensitive in the open question, she does not repeat it or ask them to expand.
03

What happens to an answer

  1. She states it and goes straight in. One question at a time, in the fixed order, waiting for each answer. A one-word reply is a complete answer, so she responds rather than waiting for more of it.
  2. Nothing is captured during the call. There is no form, no keypad entry, no extra step that could break mid-survey. The conversation is the record.
  3. Afterwards, the platform reads the transcript and fills in the answers alongside everything else it extracts about the call. It also judges separately whether she ran the survey on a call where she should have, which is how we measure her adherence rather than assume it.
  4. Our service files it with the rest of the call record. One row per call.
  5. The wave decides whether it counts. A single dated cutoff. Calls before it are kept and not counted, so a clean start does not mean deleting anything.
  6. Proportions are counted per person, words are counted per call. One tester who calls three times is one opinion in the headline, but all three of their comments are shown. This split was learned the hard way, described below.
  7. Calls needing a human look are surfaced under the headline, chosen by negative sentiment or a low score, rather than left for somebody to find.
04

Three counting rules that were learned the hard way

A respondent is a handset plus a personanot just a phone number

A small group of testers calls many times from the same phone, as different members. Counting by phone number alone collapsed all of that into one respondent and only ever read their first answer. It cost a real finding: a tester called as one member, hit a question Robin could not answer, and said they would rather do it with Robin but would wait for a person since she could not help. The headline never saw it. The same tester preferred Robin for a balance check and a person for a different topic, and those are two findings, not one opinion to average.

Counts per person, words per calltwo different rules on purpose

Proportions come from respondents, so one tester's three calls are one opinion. Comments come from calls, because the per-person view keeps only a first call and a comment left on a later one would simply disappear. That was live: the only real comment we had was on a second call, and the page showed zero comments while one existed.

A score is not the only unhappy signaltone matters too

A call is flagged for review on negative sentiment or on a low score, not on the score alone. One call rated three out of five, which is an unremarkable number, while the caller was objecting to Robin remarking that they were "well past" a certain age. Tone problems do not reliably show up as low numbers.

07

The choices behind this, and what each one cost

  • It is a paragraph, not a product. No workflow to build, no extra step in the call, no new place for the conversation to break. It comes off by deleting the paragraph. The cost is that its accuracy depends on Robin following written instructions, which is exactly why a separate judge scores whether she asked.
  • "Even if it took longer" never changes. The two groups of testers must get an identical instrument or the comparison between them is worthless. The cost is that we are stuck with the wording now, including anything we would phrase better.
  • She states it rather than asking permission. More answers, and a slightly more abrupt ending for anyone who was not expecting it. Testers were briefed; a real member would not be.
  • Never on a transfer. Somebody who wants a person gets a person, with nothing asked on the way. The cost is a blind spot exactly where dissatisfaction is most likely, which is why the declined-transfer rule matters so much.
  • A clean start by cutoff, not by deletion. Earlier calls are kept and simply not counted. The cost is that two different numbers exist for the same question depending on the window, which is a real source of confusion when somebody quotes an old figure.
  • The chart stays hidden until there are twenty respondents. A percentage from four people looks like a finding and is not one. The cost is a page that looks unfinished early on, which is the honest state.
08

What is true today, and what is not

The wave opened today, 21 September 2026. Inside it there are 15 calls so far, 7 where she ran the survey, 4 distinct respondents, and 4 written comments. Nothing is flagged for review yet. Those are the real numbers as of this writing, and they are far too small to conclude anything from.

The earlier answers are kept and do not count. Across all 123 calls ever recorded there are 29 stated preferences and 22 recommendation scores. They were given before the wave, under a mix of instrument versions, and the page deliberately ignores them. If somebody quotes a percentage from before today, that is where it came from.

The project's own written notes about this survey are out of date. They describe two questions with a one-to-five rating. The live instrument is four questions with two zero-to-ten scales. Where a document and the running agent disagree, the agent is what callers actually experienced.

One table built for this is empty and that is fine. A dedicated survey table exists with no rows in it. The answers arrive attached to the call record instead, and are read through a view. The separate path was designed and never finished, and nothing depends on it.

It is designed to be removed. Every part of it is a paragraph, a setting or a view. When the question is answered, this comes off, and the permanent way we watch quality is the reviewer, not this.
