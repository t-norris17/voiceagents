The Quality Survey

Four questions at the end of a call, and five times she must not ask them.

This settles one argument: would callers rather handle a question with Robin, or hold for a person. There is no survey software. The questions are a paragraph in her rules, the answers are read out of the transcript after the call, and the whole thing comes off by deleting the paragraph.

How she asks

  1. She states it. "Before I go, please answer the following questions." She does not ask permission, because asking invites a no that nobody needs.
  2. One at a time, in order, waiting for each answer. A one-word reply is a complete answer, so she takes it and moves on.
  3. Question one never changes wording. Without "even if it took longer" people compare Robin to an imaginary person who picks up instantly, and the answer means nothing. Both groups of testers have to get an identical question or the comparison between them is dead.
  4. She stops when they stop. Answers already given are kept. She never re-words a question and tries again, never asks why, never asks twice.
  5. She does not read anything back. No summarising, no confirming. If somebody says something sensitive in question four, she does not repeat it.
  6. The answers are read out of the transcript afterwards, along with a separate judgment of whether she ran the survey on a call where she should have.

When she says nothing at all

  • The call is ending in a transfer. Somebody who wants a person gets a person, with nothing asked on the way. A transfer she offered and they declined is not a transfer: that call counts and she asks as normal.
  • Verification failed.
  • The caller never asked anything.
  • She already asked on this call.
  • They said they are in a hurry.

How answers are counted

  • Proportions count per respondent, so one tester's three calls are one opinion. Comments count per call, so a comment left on a later call is not lost.
  • A respondent is the handset plus the member they verified as. One tester working several personas is several respondents, on purpose.
  • A call is flagged for a human look on negative sentiment or a low score. Tone problems do not reliably show up as low numbers.
  • Only calls after the wave cutoff count. Earlier ones are kept, not deleted.
