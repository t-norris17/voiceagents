The first version compared her answers to twenty-five ideal answers written by hand. When Robin moved to a different plan it carried on marking against the old plan's key and produced quality numbers nobody could see were wrong. This version has no key. It uses her own sources, so it keeps working when the plan changes.

How it works

  1. It picks up calls not yet marked, ten per run, so one click cannot become a very long wait or a large bill.
  2. It finds the pages she read. The platform records which documents were put in front of her on each turn, and which passages she drew on. It fetches exactly those.
  3. It checks every factual claim she made against those pages and records the sentence that establishes it, or reports that there is none. Its own knowledge does not count: a claim true in general but absent from her documents is marked unsupported.
  4. Balances and loan figures are left out. Those come from an account lookup rather than a document, and marking them unsupported would penalise her for being accurate.
  5. Code applies the deductions. Every answer starts at five. Contradicting a source costs three, a claim the source does not establish costs two, not answering the question costs two, a material omission costs one, and a thin answer where the source had more costs one.
  6. The page shows words, not the number. Correct, incomplete or wrong, with the reasons listed and the quotes one click away.

What it records

A verdict per answer
Each claim with its supporting sentence, or a note that there is none.
How well grounded
Grounded, unsupported, contradicted, or no source. A page we cannot read leaves the answer unscored rather than guessed at.
Every question asked
Including the ones she could not answer, with the reason: nothing covers it, it exists but she missed it, it was not a plan question, or she correctly declined.
A security check
Did she disclose personal details, or answer an account question before the caller was verified. Asked of every call.

What it cannot tell you

  • Whether a document is correct. It checks faithfulness to the source, so a wrong document produces a correct-looking mark.
  • Anything at all until somebody opens the page. There is no schedule.

The ranked list of questions Robin could not answer was a by-product of marking her, and has turned out to be the most useful thing the module produces: it says what to write next without anyone having to guess.
