What Robin is, and how she works

Robin answers the plan's phone line. This is what she is made of, what happens on a call, who checks her work, and what a caller would notice if a piece of it stopped working. No technical background is assumed, and nothing here is softened.

01

The short version

It helps to think of Robin as a new person on the phone team, hired for one job: answering questions about the Vertex Manufacturing 401(k). Like a new hire, she has been given four things, and those four things are the entire system.

  • A set of rules she has memorised. Check who is calling before answering anything. Never say a Social Security number out loud. Offer to put someone through to a person rather than give them a number to dial themselves. The rules are written in plain English, and they apply on every call.
  • A binder of approved plan documents. Six of them. She may only answer from what is in the binder. When the binder does not cover something, she says she is not certain and offers to connect the caller to a specialist. She is not allowed to fill the gap herself.
  • A phone line to the records department. She may ask it exactly two questions: is this really the person they say they are, and what are this caller's own numbers. Nothing else.
  • A supervisor who reads every call afterwards. Not a person. A piece of software that writes up what happened, and a second one that checks what she said against the binder pages she actually used.

The rest of this page is those four things in more detail, then what connects them, then what we already know is unfinished.

Where the software actually runs. Four outside companies are involved and no others. The phone number comes from Twilio. Robin herself runs at ElevenLabs, a voice AI company. The small amount of code we wrote ourselves runs at Vercel. Our records sit in a database at Supabase. That is the whole list of things that have to be working for a caller to get an answer.

02

What Robin is made of

  • Hearing and speaking are the easy parts to imagine and the easy parts to get wrong. Her voice is generated rather than recorded, which lets her adjust how a line lands: a dollar figure slows down, a question lifts at the end. There is a pronunciation list for the plan terms a generic voice would mangle.
  • Taking turns is the hardest part of a phone conversation and the one people notice when it is wrong. She begins working out an answer while the caller is still talking, so her reply arrives without an awkward pause. If she needs more than about three seconds she makes a small sound rather than going silent, because silence on a phone call reads as a dropped line.
  • Deciding what to say is the artificial intelligence. Given what she just heard, her rules, whichever playbook applies and whatever the binder says, it produces her next sentence. A call is cut off at ten minutes, whatever is happening.
  • Rules and playbooks are not the same thing. The rules apply on every call. The playbooks are four topic guides, for locked-out accounts, general plan questions, loans, and the website, and one switches on based on what the caller says. Anything that has to be true no matter what the caller asks goes in the rules, because we cannot be certain in advance which playbook will switch on.
03

What happens on a call

  1. The call arrives. We rent a phone number and point it at Robin. Changing how she behaves is a change made in a settings screen, not new software that has to be built and released. That is why she can be adjusted between one caller and the next.
  2. She finds out who is calling. She asks for the member ID and date of birth together, and sends both to our own service, which checks them against our records and answers yes or no. From that point she carries a meaningless reference code instead of the person's details. If someone could see everything Robin holds in the middle of a call, there would be no member ID and no date of birth in it.
  3. She confirms the plan. She reads the plan name back and waits for a yes before answering anything. If the caller says it is wrong, she stops and offers to connect them rather than answer from the wrong plan's rules.
  4. She answers from the binder. The binder sits with Robin, not with us, so looking something up costs no time on the call. This is the single biggest reason she does not feel slow.
  5. She looks up their own numbers, but only when the question actually needs them: balance, vested balance, the loan they hold. She cannot be interrupted while she does this, because a caller once said "okay" in the middle of a lookup, which cancelled it, and she carried on as though she had the numbers.
  6. She hands off. When something is past what she can do, she offers to put the caller through to a specialist. We treat a transfer as a good outcome, and she is told not to apologise for it or talk the caller out of it.
  7. The call ends, and at ten minutes it ends whether or not anyone is finished.
04

What happens afterwards, and how she learns

The write-upafter every call

When a call ends, Robin's platform reads the conversation back and fills in a form: what the caller wanted, which plan topic it was, how it ended, whether the ID check passed, why it was transferred if it was, how the caller sounded, and their survey answers. It sends that to our service, which confirms the message genuinely came from the platform and not from someone pretending, then files one row for the call. Sending the same call twice cannot create two rows. Every attempt is logged, including rejected ones, which is the only reason we can answer "why is that call missing".

The reviewchecking she was right

A second piece of software checks her work, and it has no answer key to mark against. It finds which binder pages she was actually shown during that call, fetches those pages, and then checks each thing she said against the sentence that supports it. Where it cannot find a supporting sentence, it says so. One honest limitation: it only runs when somebody opens the page to look, rather than on a schedule, which is worth fixing.

Changing what she knowsthe only route in

A plan document goes into a tool that turns it into question-and-answer cards. A second AI checks every claim on every card against the original document and reports where a claim has no support. Any card containing a person's name, Social Security number or account number is refused outright rather than flagged. Then it stops and waits. Only when a person approves does it become a dated, kept copy, and only then does it reach Robin's binder. Nobody can change what Robin says without a person approving it, and anything published can be corrected or switched off afterwards.

Who can see all thisone website, one sign-in

One internal website is the front door to all three: what callers said about her, whether she was right, every recent call and its transcript with the caller's own details removed, the tool for changing what she knows, and a panel showing her current settings read from the platform rather than from a document that may be out of date. It can read all of it and change none of it, and it is deliberately not part of a phone call, so if that website went down mid-morning no caller would notice.

07

The choices behind this, and what each one cost

  • Her binder lives with her, not with us. That is why she answers instantly. The price is that there are two copies of every plan document, hers and ours, and they can drift apart. They have, more than once. It is why the settings panel reads from the live system rather than from a file.
  • She is told to check ID first. She is not physically prevented from skipping it. This is the most important sentence on this page. The requirement lives in her rules, the way a procedure lives in a handbook, not as a lock she cannot open. That is true of every system of this kind today. What we do about it is measure: the ID result is recorded on every single call, and the separate review checks what she said against what she was actually shown. We would know. We are not relying on her alone.
  • She can ask our systems exactly two questions. Not "anything about a member". Two. And the answer to the second one deliberately leaves out how much a person could borrow, so that she cannot quote a borrowing limit even if a caller pushes for one.
  • The website everyone watches her through is kept outside the call. It can read everything and change nothing, and Robin has no path to it. The price is one more place where a password has to match, which has taken the website itself offline once.
  • The settings screen is the master copy, not our documents. Our written records describe what we intended. The live settings are what she is actually doing. Where the two disagree, the live settings win, and any page that claims to show you her configuration reads it live.
08

What is true today, and what is not

She is live, on invented people. Robin answers a real phone number. The 53 member records behind her, their balances and the one loan are all made up. There is no real member information anywhere in this system, by rule, and that rule is the reason we can run it at all.

The numbers start at zero. The customer wave began on 21 September 2026. Everything the dashboard reports counts only calls from that point on, so satisfaction and recommendation figures begin empty and fill up from now. Nothing on this page is a result, because there are not yet any results.

Three things she no longer uses are still switched on. Leftovers from an earlier demo: the ability to send a password reset email, an older account lookup replaced by a better one, and a case-logging step. They are harmless and they should be removed. One of the three is half-removed already.

The review only runs when someone looks. It is not on a schedule. If nobody opens the page for a week, nothing is graded for a week. That is a gap, not a design choice.

Calls are recorded and kept indefinitely. Voice recordings and transcripts are retained with no automatic deletion. That is defensible while every caller detail is invented, and it is the first thing to revisit before any real member ever calls this number.

One feature we depend on is officially unfinished. The playbooks use a capability the vendor labels early-stage and may change without much notice. Four of Robin's behaviours rest on it. It works today; it is a thing to watch rather than a thing to worry about this week.
