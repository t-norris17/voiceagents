How Robin learns something new

The Knowledge Factory is the only way anything Robin says ever changes. This is what it does to a plan document, what it refuses outright, who has to approve, and what happens when a publish goes half wrong.

the Knowledge Factory 77 documents handled so far checked against the running system, 21 Sep 2026
01

The short version

Think of it as an editorial desk sitting between a plan document and Robin's binder. Four stages, and a person in the middle of them.

  • It triages before it does any real work. A long plan document is mostly things a caller never asks about: fund tables, legal boilerplate, glossaries. A cheap first pass reads only each section's heading and a short sample, and recommends which sections are worth processing. A person unticks the rest. This is a cost decision, and it is deliberate: spend a little to avoid paying to rewrite three hundred pages nobody will ask about.
  • It rewrites what is left into answer cards. Each card is one question a member would actually ask, and the answer Robin would say out loud to them.
  • It checks its own work twice, in two different ways. First by code, for things that must never get through whatever anyone thinks. Then by a second AI, which reads every claim on every card back against the original document.
  • Then it stops and waits. Nothing reaches Robin until a person presses publish.

Where it runs. On its own website, separate from Robin, reached through the internal portal. When something is published it writes to our own records and then into Robin's knowledge base at ElevenLabs. It is not part of a phone call and cannot disturb one that is happening.

02

What it is made of

  • Why code checks first. Anything shaped like a Social Security number stops the card from being saved at all. Not a warning, not a flag on a list somebody might work through. It cannot be saved. Putting that in code rather than in a model's instructions means no amount of clever wording in a source document can talk it into passing.
  • What code merely flags. Things that are fine on a page and useless on a phone call: "see the table above", "click the gear icon", a table, a ten-digit phone number run together with no gaps, and an answer that has grown too long to say out loud. A person decides on those.
  • What the second reader does. It pulls every checkable claim out of a card and finds the exact sentence in the original document that establishes it, or reports that there is no such sentence. It also reports facts in the source that belong on this card and were left out.
  • What the second reader is not allowed to do. Give a score. It reports evidence only, and the number you see is arithmetic on what it found. This is the whole mechanism: a model can be generous with a score, and it cannot be generous with a quote that is not in the document.
03

What happens to a document

  1. The document goes in. Up to about two hundred thousand characters at a time, which is a long document but not an entire plan library. If what is uploaded is a scan, a picture of words rather than words, it says so, because there is nothing there to read.
  2. Triage. The document is split into sections, and only each section's heading and a short sample are sent for assessment, never the whole thing. It comes back with a recommendation per section and a few words of reasoning, such as "fund tables, not spoken answers". It only recommends. A person unticks what they do not want, and nothing is processed until they continue.
  3. Rewriting. What survives triage becomes answer cards: a question in a member's words, and the answer Robin would speak. This is the expensive step, which is why triage exists.
  4. The code checks. A Social Security number, or the words themselves sitting next to digits, is a hard failure and the card cannot be saved. Everything else that code can catch becomes a warning for the person to judge.
  5. The second reader checks. Every claim, traced to a quote from the original, or reported as having no support. It cannot invent support from its own general knowledge of retirement plans, and a claim that is true in the world but absent from this document is marked unsupported anyway. Drift is caught at this stage: "about five percent" where the document says six percent is marked as contradicting it.
  6. A person reads and approves. They can open any card and read the quotes behind it. Nothing goes further without this step, and there is no way to schedule around it.
  7. Publishing. A new dated version is written to our records first. Then the document is created at ElevenLabs, its index is built, and it is attached to Robin. Publishing identical text again does nothing at all. A new version supersedes the previous one and removes the old copy from Robin, so she cannot end up answering from two versions of the same article.
04

What it refuses, what it flags, and what it keeps

Refusedno judgment involved

A card containing something shaped like a Social Security number, or naming one alongside digits, cannot be saved. This is checked by ordinary code before any model is consulted, which is the point: a model can be persuaded, and a pattern match cannot. It is a hard failure, not an item on a list for somebody to work through later.

Flaggeda person judges

Cross-references to things a listener cannot see, instructions to click or tap something, tables, a run of ten digits with no separators that a synthetic voice reads badly, and an answer that has grown too long to say out loud. None of these are wrong on a page. All of them are wrong when the only way a member receives them is through a phone.

Keptevery version

Publishing never edits anything in place. Each publish writes a new dated version and marks the previous one superseded, and the stale copy is detached from Robin so she cannot answer from both. Anything published can be corrected or switched off afterwards, and the version that was live on any past date can still be read.

07

The choices behind this, and what each one cost

  • Triage before rewriting. Reading headings and samples with a cheap model, so a person can throw away the fund tables before anyone pays to rewrite them. The cost is an extra step and an extra decision for the reviewer on every long document.
  • The checker is not allowed to give a score. It returns claims and quotes, and the rating is arithmetic on those. This is the anti-flattery mechanism, and it is the same design the call reviewer uses. The cost is that the rating cannot capture anything the arithmetic does not model.
  • Personal information is refused, not flagged. The safest version of this rule is the one nobody can wave through at four in the afternoon. The cost is the occasional false alarm on a legitimate nine-digit number, which a reviewer then has to reword around.
  • Versions are added, never edited. Every publish is a new version and the old one is kept. The cost is a growing table, currently 77 rows for what a reader might think of as a couple of dozen articles.
  • Our record is written before Robin's binder. A publish that fails halfway leaves us holding an approved article that Robin does not have, which is visible and retryable. The reverse order would leave Robin answering from something we had no approved record of.
  • Nothing publishes on a schedule. There is no automatic path from a source document to a live answer. The cost is that keeping Robin current is somebody's job, and if nobody does it, nothing happens.
08

What is true today, and what is not

Nothing Robin is currently serving came through this factory. This is the single most important fact on the page. The factory holds 77 articles, 29 of them published. Not one of those 29 is one of the six documents Robin actually reads on a call today. Her six were uploaded into ElevenLabs by hand, going around this pipeline entirely. So the factory works, and the material it has produced is not what she is using.

The published articles belong to a different plan. They are the earlier INTRUST plan's content, and the newest of them was last changed on 27 July 2026. Robin now answers for the Vertex Manufacturing plan. The two sets do not overlap at all.

What that means in practice. Publishing Robin's six live documents through this factory would give us the thing we currently lack: a dated, approved, checkable record of what she is allowed to say. Until that happens, her knowledge has no audit trail on our side, and two other tools that depend on that record are working from the wrong material.

One article is approved and was never published. It has been sitting in that state since 30 July 2026. Either it was abandoned or it was forgotten, and nothing in the system will tell you which.

The console has no sign-in of its own. It is protected only by being reached through the portal. If somebody found its direct address, there is currently nothing else stopping them. This was a deliberate ordering decision, not an oversight, but it is still true today.
