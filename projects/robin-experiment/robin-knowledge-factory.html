Plan documents are written to be read. Robin needs them written to be said out loud, in answer to a question somebody asked on the phone. The Knowledge Factory does that conversion, checks the result against the original, and puts a person in front of the last step.

How it works

  1. Paste the document. Up to about 200,000 characters. A scan is refused, because a picture of words has nothing to read.
  2. Triage. It splits the document into sections and sends only each heading and a short sample, then recommends which sections hold answers a member would ask for. You untick the fund tables and the legal boilerplate before anything expensive runs.
  3. Rewrite. What you kept becomes answer cards: a question in a member's words, and the answer Robin would speak.
  4. Code checks. A card containing a Social Security number cannot be saved. Wording that will not survive being spoken is flagged for you: "see the table above", "click the gear icon", tables, a ten-digit number run together.
  5. Sourcing. A second model finds the sentence in your document behind every claim on the card, or reports that there is none. It cannot return a score, so the rating is arithmetic on what it found rather than an impression.
  6. Publish. You approve. A dated version goes to our records, then the article is created at ElevenLabs, indexed, and attached to Robin. Republishing identical text does nothing. A new version supersedes the old one and removes it from her.

What it is made of

The console
Three tabs: clean, check, publish. Reached through the portal.
Triage
A cheaper model. Recommends only; you decide.
Rewrite
A larger model. Writes the cards.
Code checks
Block personal information outright. Flag what does not speak.
Sourcing check
A second model. Quotes and gaps, never a number.
Approval
A person. Nothing reaches Robin without this step, and there is no schedule around it.
Our records
Every version kept and dated. Anything published can be corrected or switched off.
Robin's binder
The serving copy at ElevenLabs, retrieved during the call.

Robin's six current documents were uploaded to ElevenLabs by hand and did not come through here, so what she says today has no approved record on our side. Publishing them through the factory closes that gap.
