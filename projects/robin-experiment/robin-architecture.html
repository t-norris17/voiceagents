How Robin is put together

What the parts are, which of them are in a live call, and what crosses the line between one part and the next. Every configuration fact on this page was read from the live ElevenLabs agent and the live database, not from a file in the project repository.

01

The short version

Robin is not one system. She is four things that share a phone number.

  • An agent configured on ElevenLabs. Her prompt, her procedures, her knowledge and her voice all live in ElevenLabs' dashboard. There is no Robin in this repository. The platform is the system of record, which is why the portal reads her configuration live rather than rendering a file.
  • A broker. Fourteen small serverless functions on Vercel. It is the only service Robin is allowed to call while a call is happening, and the only thing that touches the database.
  • A database. Thirteen tables on Supabase: synthetic members and their loans, one row per call, the grader's scores, and the versioned record of everything she has been taught.
  • Three surfaces around her. The Knowledge Factory publishes what she knows, the grader checks what she said, and the portal is the front door to both. None of the three can affect a call that is in progress.

The rest of this page is those parts in detail, then the boundaries between them, then the decisions that put the boundaries where they are.

02

What the agent itself is made of

  • Hearing. scribe_realtime at high quality. Turn-taking is turn_v3 with speculative turns, so the model starts forming a reply before the caller has finished. A thirty-second turn timeout, and a three-second soft timeout that emits a short generated thinking sound rather than dead air.
  • Deciding. claude-haiku-4-5, temperature 0.49, ten-minute ceiling on a call. Parallel tool calls are off, so tools run one at a time and in order.
  • Speaking. eleven_v3_conversational with expressive mode on, stability 0.57, similarity 0.80, a pronunciation dictionary attached and phoneme tags enabled. English, with a Spanish preset that carries its own opening line.
  • What the model reads. The system prompt carries the rules that must hold on every call: verify before answering anything, check the account before answering a borrowing question, offer a transfer rather than a phone number, never say an SSN or a PIN. The four procedures are free-form, which is what lets them cite the knowledge base; each is matched by trigger phrases and bound to the specific documents and tools it may use. Six documents sit in the knowledge base: five are searched per question by RAG (e5_mistral_7b_instruct, at most five chunks and 8,000 characters), and the online portal guide is held in the prompt in full.
  • Why the rules live in the prompt rather than in the procedures. ElevenLabs does not document how a procedure trigger is matched. Anything that must hold whichever procedure fires, or when none does, has to be in the standing prompt. That is a deliberate duplication.
03

A call, end to end

  1. The number. A Twilio number, imported into ElevenLabs and assigned to the agent's main branch rather than pinned to a version. A save in the dashboard therefore changes what the next caller hears, with no deploy anywhere.
  2. Identity. The model calls verify_caller with the member ID and date of birth as spoken. The broker parses spoken forms, checks members, and returns {verified, subject_ref, first_name, plan_name, consented}. subject_ref is opaque: after this hop the agent holds a handle, never a member ID or a date of birth. Interruptions are disabled for the duration of this call.
  3. The plan. The model reads plan_name back to the caller and waits for a yes before answering anything. That confirmation is a prompt rule, not a platform feature.
  4. Knowledge. Retrieval runs inside the ElevenLabs runtime against the indexed documents. Nothing of ours is called, and nothing is fetched from the database. This is the single largest latency decision in the system.
  5. The caller's own figures. get_balance with the subject_ref returns balance, vested balance, the outstanding-loan flag and, when there is a loan, its full schedule from member_loans. Interruptions are disabled here too, after a caller's "okay" mid-lookup once aborted the call and left the model answering with no account data.
  6. A limit. transfer_to_number puts the caller through to a person. Skip turn lets the agent wait in silence while a caller looks something up.
  7. The end. A hard ceiling of ten minutes, after which the platform closes the call.
04

The three loops that run outside a call

The recordElevenLabs → broker → Supabase

When a call ends, ElevenLabs runs its own analysis pass over the transcript with gemini-2.5-flash: twenty-one data collection fields (intent, plan topic, outcome, transfer reason, verification result, sentiment, the five survey answers, the caller's stated name, and the rest), plus one evaluation criterion that judges whether Robin ran the survey on a call where she should have. Topic discovery and sentiment analysis run alongside. The result is posted to /api/postcall, signed with HMAC-SHA256. The broker rejects an unsigned or mis-signed payload, and upserts one row into ai_call_events, idempotent on the conversation id, so a retry cannot double-count. Every attempt, accepted or rejected, leaves a row in webhook_ingest_log, which is how "where are my calls" is answerable at all.

The gradebroker → ElevenLabs → Supabase

The grader has no answer key. For each call it reads which knowledge documents Robin actually retrieved, because ElevenLabs records the document id behind every chunk it served. It fetches those documents' text from the ElevenLabs knowledge API, caches it in kb_document_cache so a run costs one fetch per document rather than one per call, and then checks each claim she made against the sentence that supports it. Results land in call_question_scores. It grades ten calls per run and runs when someone opens the Accuracy page, which is the thing a scheduled job would fix.

The knowledgeKnowledge Factory → Supabase → ElevenLabs

A source document goes into the Knowledge Factory, which extracts answer cards, has a second model check every claim against the source text, and refuses to save a card containing a name, a Social Security number or an account number. Then it waits. On publish it writes a versioned row to kb_articles, the durable record and the audit trail, and only then creates the document in ElevenLabs, computes its RAG index and attaches it to the agent. This is the only path in the system that changes what Robin says, and a person stands in the middle of it.

The portalNext.js on Vercel

One app, one sign-in, in front of all of the above. It owns no data. A route map lists every API path it will proxy and where that path goes, the broker or the Knowledge Factory; a path not on the list is a 404, so a new surface has to be added deliberately. Proxied requests carry a shared internal header that the two upstreams accept in place of their own password. The configuration panel reads the ElevenLabs API directly, read-only, cached for sixty seconds. Robin's own endpoints are deliberately absent from the route map, so there is no path by which the portal can sit between a live call and its tools.

07

Decisions that put the lines where they are

  • Knowledge is published into ElevenLabs, not queried from us. The alternative was having the agent call our database for knowledge mid-conversation. That would add a round trip to every plan answer. The cost of the choice we made is that publishing is a two-system write, and the repository's own copies of the documents can drift from the live ones. They have, more than once. The portal's configuration panel reads live for exactly that reason.
  • The identity gate is written, not wired. Nothing in the platform prevents the model from answering before verify_caller succeeds. The gate is an instruction in the system prompt, and its compensating control is measurement after the fact: the verification result is an extracted field on every call, and the grader checks what she said against what she was given. This is the most important sentence on this page for anyone assessing risk.
  • The broker is the only thing Robin can call. Two endpoints, both narrow, both returning the minimum. Tools return an opaque handle rather than a member identifier, and get_balance deliberately does not return a borrowing limit, so the model cannot quote one.
  • The portal is outside the call path on purpose. It proxies, it does not own. Robin's endpoints are not on its route map, so no portal change and no portal outage can reach a call in progress. The cost is a second place where a secret has to match, which has already caused one outage of the portal itself.
  • Configuration is not in this repository. The dashboard is the system of record for the prompt, the procedures, the documents and the voice. The repository holds design documents and the two deployed services. Any page that claims to show Robin's configuration reads it live.
08

What is true today, and what is not

Live, on synthetic data. Robin answers a real phone number. The 53 member records, their balances and the one active loan are synthetic. There is no real member data anywhere in the system, by rule.

Results start from zero. The customer wave began on 21 September 2026. The survey views count only calls inside that wave, so the response and recommendation figures in the portal begin at zero and accumulate from now. No outcome on this page is a measured result, because there are not yet any to report.

Three tools are attached but unused. send_reset_email, get_plan_details and document_resolution remain on the agent from the earlier password-reset demo. get_plan_details is superseded by get_balance and its removal is half-done: the procedure draft no longer references it, the agent still does.

One table is empty on purpose. call_surveys has no rows. The survey answers arrive as extracted fields on ai_call_events and are read through a view; the separate parser that would fill that table was never merged.

Recording and retention are open. Voice recording is on, retention is set to indefinite, and no automatic transcript or PII deletion is configured. That is defensible while the data is synthetic and is the first thing to revisit if it ever stops being synthetic.

Procedures are an Alpha feature. ElevenLabs may change them without notice, and a free-form procedure cannot be converted to a structured one or back. Four of Robin's behaviours rest on that feature.
